# Edit Short Video

A premium edit is not the one with the most things, it is the one that points to what matters at the exact moment, with a closed system of typography, color and motion, and lets the rest breathe. Every element has a function: the zoom marks a change of idea and makes a sound, the b-roll enters when the line names it, the text names the idea instead of transcribing it and the audio is leveled. No render is spent before the user has read and approved what is going to be done, and none is shown to them before the agent has reviewed it in full.

The skill edits two formats. A **talking-head piece** starts from the creator's camera recording, and most of this file describes it. A **visual video** has no camera take: full-frame motion graphics scenes built from a base template, with the creator's voice-over or without voice; "Visual video" below says what changes for it, and `references/visual-video.md` holds the detail.

The editing rules of this skill are defaults: one coherent system that works when the brand has not decided otherwise. The brand guide (`brand-guide/`) and the workspace instructions win when they say otherwise, and the proposal states which rule they changed.

Everything the user reads (chat, questions, the proposal, `piece.md`) is written in the user's language, or the one the workspace instructions set; on-screen text is written in the language of the piece.

Paths under `scripts/` and `references/` are relative to this skill's folder; commands write it as `<skill-dir>`, to be replaced with the real path, because they run from the workspace root or from the project folder. Piece folders follow the workspace layout in the repo's `CONVENTIONS.md`.

## Flow

Each step has a gate: you do not move to the next one without what it requires. The steps are written for a talking-head piece; a visual video goes through the same seven with the differences of "Visual video" below.

1. **Ingest.**
   - Move the material to `social/<piece>/source/` with lowercase names without spaces: the to-camera take in `source/recording/`, the support material in `source/broll/` (see `social/AGENTS.md`). The original clips stay intact with their camera names and the render always starts from them.
   - `scripts/probe-media.sh` on every file (codec, resolution, fps, color space, HDR) and the `analyze-video` skill on every recorded clip.
   - Recording by sentences: follow the takes flow of `analyze-video` until the user chooses. `sentences.json` is the source of the cut.
   - If the contact sheet shows an already edited export (burned-in subtitles, music, cards), ask for the original recording and the loose b-roll. If they do not exist, the proposal states it and limits itself to what an export allows (reframing that hides the burned-in text, layers on top, no subtitling over subtitles).
   - Measure the framing of each take (see "Framing" below) and read the script: its beat map says what visual each section called for.
2. **Edit proposal** (text, no render), in `social/<piece>/edit/proposal.md` with the template in `references/proposal-template.md`.
   - Visuals: start from the direction recorded in the script. If there is none, offer the visual ideation of `brand-guide/visual-ideation.md` when the workspace has it; if the user asks for it, explore 6 to 8 directions, show them all in the chat and agree on one. Physical and 3D effects with the dose of `brand-guide/motion.md`.
   - Write the cut with timings, the resulting duration and the layer map per beat (what is seen, what text, what b-roll and with what function, what transition, what movement calls for sound), the subtitles and the showpiece effects.
   - Invoke the `find-video-audio` skill with that layer map and complete the sound section.
   - If the requested duration clashes with what the material sustains, propose the one it sustains and make it the first question.
3. **Space map**, before presenting the proposal. With the shots and their crop already decided, write `edit/space.json` and run the script below. The file holds, per beat: one real frame, the subtitle box of that section, the box of each graphic at real size with its smallest text, measured at the largest extent its animation reaches (a zoom or a push-in grows it) and, in card sections, the card edge. The format is in the script's `--help`.

   ```bash
   uv run -q --python 3.11 --with numpy --with "opencv-python-headless<5" --with "rembg[cpu]" python <skill-dir>/scripts/space-map.py social/<piece>/edit/space.json social/<piece>/edit/tests/space-map --card-bg "<dark background of the brand>"
   ```

   A beat fails if a graphic touches the face or the subtitle, if its text falls below 36 px or if it leaves the safe zone; if the subtitle covers the face or ends below y 1400; in a split view section, also if the graphic or the subtitle go below the edge (the take would end up behind the b-roll) or if the edge does not pass at eyebrow height. A beat whose frame cannot be read or whose face is not detected also fails: choose another instant of the beat, or declare `"face": false` when the creator is not in that frame. What does not fit is solved in the proposal (another place, another size, the card layout, a brand background section or removing it), never after building. A tighter framing eats the space at the sides, and a graphic shrunk to fit leaves its text illegible.
4. **Approval.** Present the proposal with `space-map.png` and ask with the structured question tool (`AskUserQuestion` in Claude Code) whatever decides the edit; iterate until an explicit yes. Then the user listens to and approves the sounds in `edit/candidates/choose-sounds.html` (from `find-video-audio`): approving the proposal does not approve the sounds. Only with the approval does the reusable material go into `media/audio/` and the chosen material into the project.
5. **Visual test.** Build the project (`references/hyperframes-recipes.md`) and take with `snapshot` three or four key frames: the hook, a beat headline, an integrated b-roll and the closing. Review them at full size like a designer (in the creator's cutout, look first at the fine edges: hair, accessories and hands), present them in a single side-by-side image and open it for the user, who approves or adjusts. If there is a detail to decide, show two or three real variants on the same frame (recipe "Design variants to decide").
6. **Review render.** Full render at 30 fps with the mixed audio over the normalized voice ("Voice level" in the recipes), passed through `scripts/final-encode.sh` at the speed of the piece, with `scripts/check-render.sh` clean. Before showing it, the full review of `references/quality-control.md`; whatever it finds is fixed all together in one more render. Open it and ask the user to watch it on the phone.
7. **Final render.** Render at delivery quality, write the deliverable with `scripts/final-encode.sh` (speed, loudness and export preset in one encode; it ends by measuring the file with `scripts/loudness.sh`) and go through the list in `references/quality-control.md` point by point. Deliver in `social/<piece>/export/<slug>-<channel>.mp4` (one file if it works for all), update `piece.md` (Edit and Audio sections, with licenses) and leave the project in `social/<piece>/edit/project/`. Transcribe the export with the `analyze-video` skill into `edit/analysis/final-video/` and add to the `analysis.md` it writes there the applied speed and the pace in words per minute: it is what remains of the piece when `source/` is cleaned after publishing.

Before opening a new version of an image or a video, close the previous one (on macOS with QuickTime: `osascript -e 'tell application "QuickTime Player" to close (every document whose name contains "<name>")'`). Ask only when the answer changes the edit; otherwise, state the assumption in the proposal and move on. This skill does not publish or schedule anything: that belongs to `schedule-social-piece` or the workspace's publishing flow, with the approval of the user.

## Visual video

A piece with no camera take. The flow keeps its seven steps and its gates; `references/visual-video.md` has the detail of each difference.

1. **Ingest.** There is no camera recording. With voice-over, the voice recording goes to `source/recording/` and through the `analyze-video` skill, which gives the time of every word. A piece with no voice has no audio to analyze: `script.md` and its screen table are the source. Images, screenshots and b-roll, if the piece has them, go to `source/broll/` and through `scripts/probe-media.sh` as usual.
2. **Proposal.** The layout is full-frame scenes, one lead visual per scene. The cut section is the voice-over cut list; when nobody speaks, it is the screen table, with the seconds each screen needs to be read. The layer map has one row per scene.
3. **Space map.** It runs at the visual test instead of before the proposal: there is no framing to measure, so it checks frames of the built composition (`"image"` beats) against the safe zone, the button column, the 36 px minimum and the subtitle.
4. **Approval.** The same: the proposal first, then the sounds.
5. **Build and visual test.** Copy `assets/visual-video/` to `edit/project/`, set the brand tokens and fonts, write the scenes. With voice-over, the cut goes through `scripts/build-cut.py single --voice-only`, which writes `voice.wav` and `cut.json`, and each scene starts on the first word of its beat. When nobody speaks, the reading time of each scene sets its length. `scripts/verify-scenes.py` checks either timing and fails loudly; then `check`, one `snapshot` per scene and the space map on those frames.
6. **Review render.** The same. With voice-over, `scripts/verify-cut.py` on `voice.wav` and `scripts/verify-sfx.py` run as in any piece with a voice. Without voice neither applies and neither is run: music is required instead, and the piece is built at its final pace and not sped up.
7. **Final render.** The same encode and the same list, with the checks and criteria that `references/quality-control.md` lists for the variant. The export of a piece without voice is not transcribed: `piece.md` records its scenes and their durations instead.

## Working without redoing

Every render and every correction round costs the user time. Failures are found before showing anything:

1. **Plan on the real framing.** No place in the layer map ("next to the head", "over the shoulder") counts until the space map checks it. If the framing changes later, repeat the space map before continuing.
2. **Measure each element where it is going to live.** An element built on its own (a sub-composition, a card tried in a separate page) is reviewed placed in the final composition, with its scale, the global styles of `index.html` and the project fonts; reviewing it alone proves nothing (see "Composition traps" in the recipes).
3. **Batch and verify before rendering.** The changes of a round are all applied and checked with `check`, the space map and a `snapshot` of everything touched; only then is there a render. Never one render per adjustment.
4. **Review like a designer before showing.** The render review is done by the agent. The user decides taste and judgment; it is not their job to find covered text, erased syllables or music that disappears.
5. **"Solved" only with a conclusive measurement.** Every solved point carries the measurement that proves it (the voice level at the start of a line, the music level against the clip without music, a frame). If the measurement is not conclusive, say so and look for another.
6. **Validate an automatic check with a provoked failure.** Before trusting a script's "OK", test it with a case that must fail and another that must pass. A check that could not read its input, or that found nothing to check, is a failure: the scripts of this skill exit with a non-zero code in both cases.
7. **Do not build tools in the middle of an edit.** A verification is solved first with what exists (frames, crops, ffmpeg measurements); a new tool is proposed for later. A half-built tool that gives false "OK"s is worse than not having it.
8. **Stop when something clashes.** If a fix opens a new problem twice in a row, stop, explain the root cause and propose another path instead of continuing to patch.

## Deciding the edit

1. **Improvisation counts.** By default, what the creator says differently from the script is treated as a choice, not as a mistake: it is compared with the written version for what it adds (rhythm, energy, naturalness, information) and what it costs (seconds, clarity), without preferring the script by default. A new fact or claim is verified with the workspace's sources; only if it cannot be checked, ask the user.
2. **Cut.**
   - Recording by sentences: the segments are the chosen takes with their `cuts` from `sentences.json`, in script order; the gap between sentences comes only from those cuts, never from an added gap or a frozen frame. A pause the script asks for is written as `pause` in its row and runs on real image of the same take, only while the creator stays still and looking at the camera (`analyze-video/references/sentence-recording.md`). If a cut moves, check it again with `analyze-video/scripts/check-cuts.py`. The proposal names the chosen take of each sentence and the pauses.
   - Single take: with the word-level transcript (`words.json`), remove long silences leaving tight gaps of about 100 ms of the take itself, most filler words and all false starts and repetitions; keep breaths with a function and room tone. Never cut mid-syllable: a quiet moment is not always silence. A plosive closure or an unstressed final syllable (the "-pact" of "impact") can sit 20 to 30 dB under the vowel, and Whisper's word times can be off by a syllable. Between two words said without a pause, measurements alone do not find the edge reliably: cut three or four candidates spaced 40 ms apart around the start Whisper gives for the next word, and have the user hear them before fixing the cut. As in a recording by sentences, no expression after a sentence is shown (mouth, eyes, body): the cut goes before it. The result is a cut list: the `[start, end]` of each segment in the original, with its sentence.
   - Both paths end in the same two files, written by `scripts/build-cut.py` (`master` for a recording by sentences, `single` for a cut list) and measured by `scripts/verify-cut.py`: `clip.mp4`, the cut master, and `cut.json`, the time of every segment and every word on that master ("Cut master" in the recipes). Subtitles, sounds and checks are computed from `cut.json`.
   - Ending: the last word is followed by about 0.5 s of real image before the hard cut. In a recording by sentences that is a `pause` of 0.5 in the last row; in a single take, the last segment ends 0.5 s after the last word. If the brand guide defines a closing gesture, the video ends on the complete gesture with nothing added after it; `analyze-video/references/sentence-recording.md` describes how that cut is placed.
3. **Framing.**
   - The frame holds what the brand guide or the user wants in it, and nothing else. At ingest, look at a frame of each take with a grid and note where anything that should stay out begins (the edge of a desk, a light stand, how much of the creator's body shows). The widest shot is a crop of the original, at its original resolution, that leaves it out, and that limit holds in every section, including a shrunken cutout over a brand background and a series intro. What is not written in the brand guide is decided in the proposal.
   - From that shot, two sizes from the same take, alternated at the cuts and with the change where the idea changes: 100 % and a punch-in. Never two consecutive cuts at the same size. Eye line in the upper third.
   - Everything that enters the piece keeps its maximum quality: the creator's recording, the b-roll, the photos and the images. No punch-in, push-in, zoom or scale enlarges a pixel beyond its source when the source has the pixels. The master is built from the original at the canvas size times the largest scale the composition applies to the camera, never above the original (1296x2304 for punch-ins up to 120 %; a 4K original allows up to 200 %), and the composition shows it at 1080x1920. An original at canvas size (1080x1920) has no spare pixels: its master stays at 1080x1920 and its punch-ins are limited to 110 to 115 %, the one enlargement this skill accepts. B-roll clips are prepared at CRF 10 at the size they are shown at their largest; photos and images enter as lossless WebP or PNG, at the resolution of the original. The fewest encodes possible: master at CRF 10, and the speed change goes in the same pass as the final encode ("Preparing the clips", "Cut master" and "Speed and final encode" in the recipes).
4. **Beats and layers.** Only text the voice does not say ("Text that does not repeat the voice" in `references/visual-system.md`): a beat headline if it adds an idea or a fact that is not heard, a pinned term if a concept will repeat, a "Fact" or "Opinion" pill when the script separates them, a list that builds if there is an enumeration and one animated figure at most. In addition, one key phrase with animated typography every 20 to 25 s, with its subtitle hidden while it is on screen.
5. **Screen layout.** One per piece, written in the proposal ("Screen layouts" in `references/visual-system.md`). By default, the creator in frame and the visuals in the free space around or behind them. With a lot of b-roll or little free space, the split view (card with the visual on top). In both, visual-only full-screen moments when they add something.
6. **B-roll with a function.** It enters at the second the line names what it shows, lasts 3 to 5 s and has a function from the closed list (illustrate, show the demo, compare, establish the location, cover a cut). Full screen if it is more informative than the face, floating screenshot if it is brief, picture in picture in demos, behind the creator cut out only if it is context without text to read. When the user asks for a shot by its content, locate it and confirm file and second with a frame before placing it.
7. **Showpiece effects, with a quota.** Brand background with the creator cut out: two or three sections (hook, strong headline). Giant typography: one per video, with a verified figure. Zoom into the screen: once or twice, in demos, without cutting the text. Parallax: only over static screenshots longer than 4 s, with small angles.
8. **Sound.** Every punch-in, shot change and animated zoom carries a sound, subtle and short; emphasis, a low hit; block transitions, a whoosh. Cut hits are the sound signature and do not count; on top of them, between three and six featured sounds per piece. A cut hit attacks on the frame of the cut and its tail ends before the first syllable of the new sentence. Every effect stays at least 6 dB under the voice in a word it overlaps, and a cut hit at least 10 dB, checked with `scripts/verify-sfx.py`. Sounds come from `find-video-audio` and are respected exactly as the user approved them.
9. **Music.** Only free tracks or tracks generated locally, without lyrics or high BPM, about 20 dB below the voice and absent in dense explanations or sensitive topics. A bed with several levels or looping is built into a single file with ffmpeg ("Music bed" in the recipes). In a sketch, cutting it dead can be the punchline.
10. **Speed.** Default, which the brand guide or the workspace instructions can change: up to 30 s at normal speed, not sped up; above 30 s, the video goes at 110 %, or the speed the brand guide sets, with the pitch intact, applied to the full render after HyperFrames ("Speed and final encode" in the recipes). A visual video without voice is built at its final pace and is never sped up.
11. **What is approved does not move without asking.** A new element does not relocate or remove an already approved one; if they clash, show the conflict and ask.
12. **A fix applies to every case.** What is fixed at one point is applied to all analogous cases in the piece and to all its formats.
13. **The approver's ideas are answered with an opinion.** Before applying an idea from the user, say whether it improves the piece, what it costs and whether there is a better option. Nothing is taken as approved without an explicit yes.
14. **Batches, one at a time.** With several videos, each one is finished and reviewed before starting the next.
15. **Format.** Apply the per-format adjustments of `references/visual-system.md` (educational, demo, opinion, progress update, sketch, community or event, visual video).

## Rules

- Fonts: only the brand's display and text fonts, loaded through the project's copy of `brand-guide/assets/fonts/fonts.css`; verify in the render that they loaded. Colors: only the palette of `visual-identity.md`, mapped to the roles of `references/visual-system.md` ("Brand tokens").
- No glitch, flash transitions, stock transition packs, cross dissolve between two takes of the same sentence, zoom on every phrase, emojis in headlines or red clickbait circle. By default there is no opening card, no outro and no logo as a signature or a corner mark, and the only logo in the video is the faint one inside the brand background, when the brand has one; a brand guide that defines an intro, a closing card or a mark wins.
- At most two simultaneous graphic elements besides the creator; subtitle and key term never animated at the same time.
- All text that is not a subtitle has 6 words at most and names the idea, it does not transcribe it; in a visual video where nobody speaks the screens carry the script, with the limits of `references/visual-video.md`. No on-screen text below 36 px on the 1080 canvas.
- Safe zone, measured on Instagram and used as the conservative default for every platform: essential content across the full width with a 60 px margin and between y 210 and 1680; the subtitle box never ends below y 1400; nothing essential in the button column (x 945 to 1080, y 1095 to 1850). Figures and reasons in `references/visual-system.md`.
- Subtitles, by default: one line of 2 to 4 words at 64 px, without one-word blocks, below the face with at least 40 px of space under the chin while the creator is on camera; position and exceptions in "Subtitles" of `references/visual-system.md`. By default the hook has no subtitles: its text is animated typography over a moving frame ("Opening and closing" in `references/visual-system.md`). If a framing leaves no room below the face, explain it and propose the way out before rendering.
- Color: everything in Rec. 709. If a clip is HDR, render with `--sdr` and advise that the next recording be made with HDR video turned off in the camera settings.
- Do not invent b-roll: what is missing is requested or marked as pending. Do not use material from `media/references/`.
- Everything generated for the piece lives in its folder: analysis in `edit/analysis/`, takes in `edit/takes/`, audio candidates in `edit/candidates/`, frames, maps and test renders in `edit/tests/`, the project in `edit/project/`. Only the session's helper scripts go to a scratch folder.
