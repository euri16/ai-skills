# Captions by platform

Checked on 2026-11-20. Every statement names the page it was read on that day. Instagram's and TikTok's own pages cannot be read without signing in, so their statements come from the secondary source named next to them, and whatever no source confirms is written as a recommendation of this skill, not as a platform rule. Check the limits again before stating them if more than six months have passed.

## Common ground

- **The caption works in search, not in virality.** In the recommendation feed interactions weigh; in search what weighs is that the text matches what is searched. YouTube says search relevance looks at "how well the title, tags, description, and video content match your search query" ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16090438?hl=en)); the Instagram and TikTok sections give what was found for each.
- **First line.** Instagram's @creators account says only the first two lines of a caption are visible before "more" (as reported by [Social Media Today, 2026-08-23](https://socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-stresses-the-importance-of-captions/828554/)). No platform publishes how many characters are visible; the figures that circulate (125, 55, 210, 140) come from blogs. If precision is needed, measure with a screenshot on the phone.
- **Length.** There is no data-backed rule: the studies are correlational and about brand accounts ([Socialinsider, 2026-10-27](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-caption-length-best-practices/): on Reels, 30 to 90 words slightly better; on TikTok, under 30 words better). That is why the skill's rule is functional: the first line does the work and the rest only goes in if it adds something.
- **No data by language.** No study found separates captions by language.

## Instagram

- Maximum **5 hashtags** per post or Reel: "Starting today, Instagram will allow up to 5 hashtags in a reel or post" ([@creators on Threads, 2025-12-18](https://www.threads.com/@creators/post/DSalXGPCWM4)). The [Graph API reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/instagram-platform/instagram-graph-api/reference/ig-user/media) still says 30: apply 5 also when publishing via API.
- Hashtags describe the content; they are not a way to reach more people. This is the skill's reading of what Instagram's head has said about hashtags, with no quote verified.
- Captions "tell search engines what your content is about" (@creators, as reported by Social Media Today in the article above). Public posts and Reels from professional accounts can appear in Google and Bing (as reported by [The Keyword, 2025-07-01](https://www.thekeyword.co/news/instagram-to-let-google-and-bing-index-public-posts-from-professional-accounts)). Recommendation of this skill: say the search words in natural sentences, and do not let an emoji stand in for a word.
- Main feed signals: "The top three signals that matter most for ranking are watch time, likes and sends", with sends "slightly more important for unconnected content" (Adam Mosseri, as quoted by [Social Media Today, 2025-01-22](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)). No data found shows that a send CTA moves them.
- Instagram asked creators not to "hack" engagement by asking people to comment a specific word, number or emoji (@creators, as quoted by [Social Media Today, 2024-06-05](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-clarifies-advice-on-single-word-ctas-and-longer-reels/718151/)); its caption advice proposes questions and inviting people to save for later. Recommendation of this skill: no long caption unrelated to the content.
- Caption of up to 2,200 characters; alt text is not supported on Reels (Graph API reference above).

## TikTok

- Search weighs how well the content matches the term entered (TikTok's Help Center, as paraphrased by [Relevant Audience](https://www.relevantaudience.com/tiktok-ads/tiktok-seo/)); in For You, interactions weigh.
- Recommendation of this skill: relevant keywords in the video, the subtitles and the caption, and never a stack of unrelated keywords.
- Maximum **5 hashtags** per post: users have seen the limit since mid 2025 and TikTok has not documented it ([Social Media Today, 2025-08-17](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/tiktok-implements-five-hashtag-limit-per-post/757857/)); Buffer applies it ("Limit of 5 hashtags per post", [Buffer Help](https://support.buffer.com/article/559-using-tiktok-with-buffer)). Caption of a video posted via API: "The maximum length is 2200 in UTF-16 runes" ([Content Posting API](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/content-posting-api-reference-direct-post)).

## YouTube Shorts

- The **title** is what is seen and it weighs in search (YouTube Help page in "Common ground"). Title of at most 100 characters and description of at most 5,000 bytes, neither with `<` or `>` ([YouTube Data API](https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/docs/videos)); in UTF-8 an accented letter takes 2 bytes and an emoji 4.
- "Up to three hashtags that are considered most engaging will appear by your video title"; with more than 60, YouTube ignores every hashtag ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658?hl=en)). That page does not ask for `#Shorts`.
- "It's okay to ask viewers to like, comment, or subscribe"; misleading titles, thumbnails and descriptions are not allowed ([spam policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973?hl=en)).

## LinkedIn (video)

- The feed uses systems that "better understand what a post is actually about" and works to reduce "comment to agree" prompts and "videos that don't match the text" ([LinkedIn, 2026-03-12](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)): the text describes what the video shows.
- Hashtags are "a nice to have, not a need to have" (Laura Lorenzetti of LinkedIn News, as quoted by [Buffer, 2025-12-19](https://buffer.com/resources/linkedin-algorithm/), which suggests three or fewer); 0 to 3.
- Vendor data ([MagicPost](https://magicpost.in/studies/linkedin-comment-bait), 684,000 posts from 16,000 creators; the keyword comparison rests on a few hundred posts): since late July 2026, "Comment KEYWORD" posts reach 62 % fewer people than the author's average post, a genuine closing question stays where it was (about 4 % below average) and so does a link in the first comment (about 11 % below).
- In video, text length barely changes engagement (Socialinsider article in "Common ground", company pages).
