# Hook library

Spoken formulas, devices and the rubric for writing hooks. Each formula executes one of the templates of `hook-templates.md`, which explains why the mechanism works; this file is for writing. The evidence behind the mechanisms and the origin of the formulas are in `evidence.md`.

**Hyper-specificity.** The more concrete the detail, the more the person recognizes themselves: talking to one person in a precise situation ("if you've ever reread your own email three times after sending it…") beats talking to an audience ("if you worry about how you write at work…"). A hook that sounds like a human moment said with precision tends to beat one that sounds like a hook.

A well-known formula is not a problem. The problem is generic execution: no concrete subject, no real data, no contrast or with a promise the video does not keep. Copy the structure, never the content.

## Spoken formulas

Write them in the language of the video. The "Template" column gives the code of the template each formula executes; a candidate is recorded with that code. The formulas are common structures of short-video openings, written here in this skill's own words; the examples are invented for several niches and only show the structure.

| # | Formula | Template | Structure | Example | When it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V1 | Object or result first | B1 | "This [object] is actually [something unexpected]" / "This is [X]. It does [Y]." | "This cutting board I have here is actually a scale." | What is shown is neither odd nor desirable |
| V2 | News with a concrete subject | B4 | "[Concrete subject] just [fact]. [Big detail]." | "A city just banned gas stoves in new homes. Here is what changes in your kitchen." | Old news, vague subject, headline bigger than the fact |
| V3 | Context, "but" and twist | A2 | "[What everyone accepts]. But [watch out for this]. [The opposite, within the topic]." | "Everyone tells you to drink water before a run. But the timing matters more than the amount." | Context nobody shares; forced twist; the first line has to stand on its own |
| V4 | Yes-question about the viewer | A1 | "How much / which [fact about you]?" + typical answer + contrast | "How many subscriptions are you paying for? Five, six… I found eleven." | Generic question ("did you know…?") or an answer that takes more than 2 s |
| V5 | Identity filter | D1 | "If [concrete condition], this is for you. The most important thing is [rule]." / "Stop scrolling if [concrete problem]." | "If you're learning to bake bread, stop following the clock." | "If you're an entrepreneur, watch this": generic; reduces reach in mixed audiences |
| V6 | I explain X in N | B3 | "[X] explained in [N] steps/levels. Level one: …" | "Compound interest explained in three steps." | Filler number |
| V7 | Stop doing X / the mistake | C2 | "If you want [result], don't do [the usual], do [something else]" / "The mistake [I used to make] with [X]" | "A huge mistake I used to make with cast iron…" | Invented mistake; scolding tone. Better in first person, only if it is true |
| V8 | X versus Y | C4 | "[A] versus [B]" / "When to use [A], [B] and [C]" | "I own a blender, a food processor and a stick blender, and each one wins at one thing." | Hidden verdict or "it depends" |
| V9 | I tested it / experiment | E3 | "I tested it so you don't have to" / "I did [X] for [N]" / "I asked the same question two ways" | "I meal-prepped every Sunday for a month so you don't have to guess." | The experiment was not really done |
| V10 | Let's do the math | B4 | "[Fact with a public figure]. Let's do the math." | "This gym sells a lifetime membership for the price of two years. Let's do the math." | Unverified figure; trivial math |
| V11 | Cultural reference | F3 | "[Celebrity, brand or event] just [something about your topic]" | "A famous chef just admitted to using store-bought stock." | Does not connect with the point or is not recognized by the audience |
| V12 | Contrarian / the truth about | C1 | "What they told you about [X] is wrong" / "Unpopular opinion: [real stance]" / "The real reason [belief] doesn't work" | "What they told you about preheating the oven is wrong." | A stance the video does not back with evidence |
| V13 | What nobody tells you | A3 | "Nobody tells you [something concrete] about [topic]" | "Nobody tells you how loud a dishwasher is in an open kitchen." | Trivial secret or one that is told everywhere |
| V14 | Regret | E1 | "I wish I had known [X] before [Y]" | "I wish I had known this before I painted my first wall." | Only if it is true for the creator |
| V15 | Story with a twist | E1 | "I almost [mistake] until [twist]" / "[Time] ago I was [situation]; today [result]" | "I almost bought the extended warranty until I read page four." | Invented anecdote |
| V16 | Data point with amplifier | B4 | "[Verified data point]." + second line that compares or twists it | The piece's verified figure, then "That is more than [comparison]." | Data point without a source; data point without a second line |
| V17 | Look at this / demonstration | A5 | "Wait, let me show you something" / "Look at what happens here" | "Look at what happens when I press this dough." | No strong visual reveal right after |
| V18 | Quick value | B2 | "[N] [things] that [concrete benefit]" / "How to [result] without [pain]" | "Three stretches that loosen your hips before a long drive." | Vague benefit ("it changes your life") |
| V19 | Future | A2 | "This is how [X] is going to be [concrete]" | "This is how you will pay at the supermarket in two years." | An abstract future with nothing to show |
| V20 | What if…? | C1 | "What if [desired result] didn't need [cost or effort taken for granted]?" | "What if a tidy kitchen didn't need a single new cabinet?" | An assumption the audience does not hold; no concrete path afterwards |
| V21 | Two things that should not go together | C1 | A state and an action that clash in one breath: "[How I am]. [What I am about to do anyway]." | "I have never kept a plant alive. Today I am repotting six." | The clash is decoration and the video never resolves it |
| V22 | A long span, compressed | C4 | "[A long stretch of real work] in [the length of this video]" | "Eight months of learning to sew, in forty seconds." | A span that was not really lived, or a comparison with nothing to show for it |
| V23 | The scene after the fix | D2 | "POV: [a recognizable moment in which the problem is already solved]" | "POV: the suitcase closes on the first try." | A moment nobody recognizes, or the way to get there never arrives |

With care, because of their closeness to hype: "This is the [craziest] thing I've seen today" (only with an exceptional visual and better without the superlative) and urgency ("You need to hear this today").

## Visual, text and sound devices

| # | Device | How it is done |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Start mid-action | Record the most interesting moment first and start there, with no intro |
| P2 | Object in hand or result on screen | From frame 0, in the central third |
| P3 | Split screen | Screenshot, footage or interface on top and the face below |
| P4 | Large text in frame 0 | Bold, high contrast, with the same terms that are spoken; length in step 3 of "Choose the hook" in `SKILL.md` |
| P5 | Visual "wait" | Something big about to happen: an object about to fall, a screen about to load |
| P6 | Unexpected zoom or cut | Micro zoom of 0.2 to 0.5 s, or a cut with the first clip under 0.5 s |
| P7 | Turn the camera to reveal | From selfie to the object, synced with the key word |
| P8 | Imperfect first frame | Keep the natural second before "being ready" |
| P9 | POV | Camera at eye level and "POV:" on screen |
| P10 | Before and after | The transformation at the start, in split screen or with a cut |
| P11 | Sound with gesture | An SFX synced with the gesture or with the entry of the text |
| P12 | Voice contrast | Start mid-sentence, drop or raise the tone |

In the editing system of `edit-short-video`, the opening is a live hook: no subtitles, animated typography synced with the voice, camera movement and the piece's visual world (or darkened b-roll) moving from frame 0 ("Opening and closing" in `edit-short-video/references/visual-system.md`). The devices in this table combine with that rule, they do not replace it.

## Rubric

Each candidate is scored from 0 to 2 on each criterion (maximum 16). Recommend only what scores 13 or more and never one with 0 in "Open unknown"; if nothing reaches it, write more candidates rather than lowering the bar.

| Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Topic clear in 1 to 2 s | It is unclear what it is about | It can be guessed | Clear without audio |
| Specificity | Generic | Something concrete | Concrete subject, action and result |
| Contrast or tension | None | Implicit | Explicit before and after, twist or conflict |
| Open unknown | The line already answers itself | A weak doubt remains | Leaves a question or unknown that forces you to stay and that the video closes |
| Aligned layers | They contradict each other | Two out of three | What is seen, read and said name the same thing |
| Truth | Promise the video does not keep or data without a source | True with a pending nuance | Verified and delivered in the video |
| Creator's voice | Generic creator template | Neutral | The creator would say it as is on camera |
| Speed to value | Value arrives late | Arrives past the middle | A proof or answer before second 10 |
