# Instagram and short video

Channel guide for Instagram Reels, which also covers the same video on TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Read by `recommend-video-hook`, `write-short-video-script` and `write-caption`.

Content language: English, plain and international (see `../brand-guide/voice-and-humor.md`).

## Goal

The viewer leaves knowing why one thing happens in their oven and what to do about it, and wants to save the video for their next bake.

## Base structure

1. **Frame 0 is the bake.** The failure, the side-by-side or the cross-section is on screen from the first frame, with three to five words of on-screen text that ask the question.
2. **The question, out loud.** One line that names what the viewer is looking at and why it is strange.
3. **The setup.** What is being compared and the one thing that differs, in a sentence.
4. **The mechanism.** One cause, in plain words, while the footage shows it.
5. **The proof.** The reveal: the cut, the measurement, the taste reaction if it really happened.
6. **The fix.** What to change next time, with exact quantities or temperatures on screen.
7. **The limit.** When this does not apply, in one sentence, if leaving it out would mislead.
8. **One useful CTA,** before the last line: save it for the next batch, try the test at home, or answer a specific question in the comments.

## Length

- Target 30 to 60 seconds. Experiments with a long reveal can go to 90 seconds.
- Cut anything that does not serve the question from frame 0.

## Production

- Shot at the counter, the bake and the creator's hands in frame; the creator's face when they explain or react.
- Overhead shots for side-by-sides, so both samples are the same size.
- Labels "Test" and "Control" on every comparison, as `../brand-guide/visual-identity.md` describes.
- Quantities and temperatures on screen in both units.
- Subtitles always; check legibility on a phone before approving.
- Write to be spoken. The hook and the payoff are written word for word; the middle can be said in the creator's own words.

## Captions

- The first line asks the question people would search for ("Why are my cookies flat?").
- The body adds what the video did not have time for: the second cause, the exact ratios, the test conditions.
- One CTA. Three to five hashtags about the bake or the science behind it (`#bakingscience`, `#pancakes`), never a block of generic ones.
- Credit any music that needs attribution, as the piece record's Audio section says.

## TikTok and YouTube Shorts

- Same video, same cover.
- TikTok: the Instagram caption, with the first line checked for the words people search on TikTok.
- Shorts: the title is the question as people would type it, under 100 characters; the description is the caption.

## Do not

- Open with a greeting, a logo or "Did you know".
- Show a result that was not the real result of the test.
- Promise "perfect every time" or a "secret".
- Give food safety advice beyond pointing to official guidance.
- Turn a comment from a viewer into the joke.
