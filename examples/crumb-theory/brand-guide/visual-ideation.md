# Visual ideation

An optional brainstorm, in text, of how a piece could look before anyone proposes visuals: the "Visual" column of a script, the layer map of an edit proposal or a cover concept. Offered by `write-short-video-script`, `edit-short-video` and `design-reel-cover`; it happens only if the user wants it, either on request or after a skill offers it. Otherwise the skill goes straight to its visual proposal.

## Why it exists

Baking science is invisible: gas, proteins and starch work inside an opaque batter. The first visual idea is usually a generic one (bubbles, a beaker, a molecule). The good one makes the invisible thing look like something the viewer already understands, without bending the science.

Reference example: for "double-acting baking powder releases gas twice, once when wet and again in the oven", the first idea was a bubble icon. The chosen direction was a two-stage rocket: stage one fires at mixing, stage two at the oven door, and the oven window becomes the launch window for the rest of the video.

## Process

1. **Write what the eye must understand.** For each beat or slide, one sentence about what the viewer should see, not what is said. "The soda batter has almost no acid to react with", not "beat 3".
2. **Write 6 to 8 directions** spread across these categories, covering every one of the first four:
   - **The real thing, up close.** The actual bake, filmed so the mechanism shows: a macro of bubbles in batter, the crumb under raking light.
   - **Side by side.** Two samples that differ in one thing, in the same frame.
   - **Everyday analogy.** Something outside baking that behaves the same way (a balloon, a sponge, a rocket), with one sentence on where the analogy stops being true.
   - **Change of scale.** Inside the dough: the gluten net, a single gas bubble, a starch granule swelling.
   - **Notebook annotation.** The real footage with drawn labels, arrows and measurements from the icon set.
   - **Ingredients as characters.** Baking soda waiting for an acid, yeast clocking in at the right temperature. Only if every action the character takes is true.
3. **Look for one world for the whole piece.** A direction that can carry several beats (and reuse an image already in the script) beats a different graphic on every beat.
4. **Rich is not loud.** A strong visual world mostly arrives with the plain rise-in entrance from `motion.md`. The single physical moment a piece is allowed is kept for the result.
5. **Filter last.** With all directions written, check them against:
   - The science: no analogy that teaches something false.
   - Honesty: no staged or generated result shown as a real one.
   - The palette and accent rule of `visual-identity.md`, and the principles of `motion.md`.
   - On-screen text that adds what the voice does not say, instead of repeating it.
   - No generic lab clip art (bubbling neon beakers, orbiting atoms) unless the piece is literally about that.
   - What the creator can film in a home kitchen with the means at hand.
6. **Around the creator.** In video, the visuals live in the free space around the creator and the bake, or behind a cutout; a full-frame visual moment is welcome when it adds something (the cross-section, the side-by-side). Placement details are in `edit-short-video`.
7. **Series reuse their kit.** An episode of a series planned in `editorial/series/<series>/series.md` starts from that series' visual kit, and whatever new element earns its place is added to the kit.

## Presenting

- Put all the directions in the chat, sorted under their categories with a single line apiece; the user picks from the full set, not a shortlist.
- Flag your pick and give its reason in a sentence. When the pick is a world for the whole piece, sketch beat by beat where it shows up.
- Never ask for a choice before the directions are visible in the chat.
- Write the chosen direction into the document that holds the piece's visuals: `script.md`, the edit `proposal.md`, or `piece.md` under `## Cover`.
