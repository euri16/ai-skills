# Positioning

What Crumb Theory covers, what it promises and what it stays away from. Read by `research-content-topic` to judge whether an idea fits the brand and which angle to take, and by any skill that needs the brand's territory before proposing a piece.

## Territory

The science behind everyday home baking, shown through small experiments in a normal kitchen: why a dough rises or stays dense, what leaveners, flour, fat, sugar, eggs and heat actually do, and which pieces of baking advice hold up when you test them.

## Promise

After a Crumb Theory video, your next bake goes better because you understand why, not because you memorized one more rule.

## What the viewer walks away with

Each piece aims for at least one of these:

- Diagnose a failed bake from what it looks like: a dense crumb, flat cookies, a sunken middle, a gummy layer.
- Swap an ingredient knowing what will change and what to adjust.
- Read a recipe and spot the step that actually matters.
- Tell a kitchen myth from a habit that has a real reason behind it.
- Run a small side-by-side test at home instead of trusting the internet, this channel included.

## Differentiators

- **One variable, on camera.** The signature format is two bowls, two pans or two loaves that differ in exactly one thing, filmed side by side.
- **The mechanism, in plain words.** Chemistry is named only when the name helps, and always explained in half a sentence.
- **Failures stay in the video.** A collapsed cake is evidence, not a blooper to cut.
- **Honest about variation.** Ovens, flours, altitude and humidity change results. The brand says so instead of promising "perfect every time".
- **No recipe treadmill.** Recipes appear only as the setting for an explanation.

## What Crumb Theory is not

- Not a recipe channel. A piece can include a recipe, but the reason to watch is the why.
- Not a nutrition or health channel. No claims about weight, digestion, blood sugar or "healthier" swaps.
- Not a food safety authority. Safety questions (raw flour, raw eggs, storage, allergens) point to official guidance; see `../operations/research-policy.md`.
- Not a product review channel. A brand of flour or an appliance appears only when the test needs it.

## Independence

Ingredients are named generically (all-purpose flour, instant yeast, double-acting baking powder) unless a specific product changes the result. Any gifted product, sponsorship or affiliate link is disclosed in the piece and never decides the outcome of a test.

## Goal for the next year

Build a library of short explainers that people save and come back to before they bake. Saves, shares and the quality of the questions in the comments count as much as follower growth.
