# Research policy

What has to be verified before a Crumb Theory piece goes out, how, and against which sources. Read by `research-content-topic`, and applied by every skill that writes a claim into a script or caption.

## What needs checking

- Every claim about a mechanism: what an ingredient does, why a dough behaves a certain way.
- Every number: temperatures, ratios, times, percentages, substitution amounts.
- Anything that touches food safety, allergens or storage.
- Claims about a product, a tool or an ingredient's composition, which can change between brands and over time.

What does not need an outside source: the result of the creator's own test, as long as the method is shown and the claim stays inside what the test covered ("in this oven, with this flour"); and opinions labeled as opinions ("I prefer").

The agent's own knowledge is a starting point, not a source. Each claim is matched to a source from the list below, and the piece's claims ledger notes the source and the day it was read. A claim with no source found is dropped, or kept only with its uncertainty said out loud.

## Source priority

1. Food science textbooks and peer-reviewed food science research.
2. University food science departments and extension programs, and government food safety agencies.
3. Technical data from ingredient manufacturers, for facts about a specific product.
4. Established baking references and test kitchens that publish their method.
5. Forums, comments and social posts: only as signals of what people believe or experience, labeled as such.

Open and read the source itself. A search snippet or an AI summary is never the evidence.

## Kitchen test protocol

A test shown as evidence follows these rules, and the script states them briefly:

- **One variable.** Split one batch where possible; same pan, same rack position, same oven, same time.
- **Measure, don't eyeball.** Weigh ingredients in grams; check the oven with an oven thermometer, not the dial.
- **Repeat.** Run the test at least twice before claiming a result. If the two runs disagree, say so or do not publish the claim.
- **Record.** Photograph both samples side by side and in cross-section, with labels, before tasting.
- **Claim small.** Say what this test showed under these conditions. General statements come from the sources above, not from one kitchen.

## Claims ledger

For each piece, the research notes keep one row per claim:

| Claim | Type | Source | Checked on | Confidence | Condition or caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Type is one of: mechanism, measured in our test, rule of thumb, opinion.

## Food safety and health

- Never go beyond official guidance on raw flour, raw dough, raw or undercooked eggs, storage times, cooling or allergens. Point the viewer to their local food safety agency.
- No nutrition, weight or medical claims of any kind.
- When a piece touches safety, the humor drops and the wording is checked against the official source word by word.

## Attribution and licenses

- Link or name the source of a non-obvious claim in the caption or a pinned comment when there is room.
- Credit recipes adapted from someone else, and never present another creator's test as the brand's own.
- Check the license of music, sound effects, images and footage before using them; record it in the piece's Audio section and in `../media/audio/log.md`.
- Disclose generated images and AI assistance when the platform asks for it or when it matters for trust.

## Independence

Ingredients and tools are judged by what the test shows, not by who sent them. Any relationship with a brand is disclosed in the piece.
